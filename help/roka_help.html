First time using Roka

What this program is, and the order to do things in.

Roka's Resonance keeps track of the things a music teacher has to keep track of: which student has which school instrument, what is broken, what it costs, who is in which class, what the library holds, and what is happening at the next concert. Everything lives on your own computer.

The ? button

Every main window has a ? in its top right corner. It opens this guide at the section for the screen you were looking at. You can also press F1 anywhere in the program.

The order to set things up

  1. Answer the few questions on the first run screen (your name, your school, and whether you teach band, choir, orchestra or elementary). This sets up your class list for you.
  2. Bring in your existing data if you have any, from CutTime, Charms or Synergy. See Bringing in your old data.
  3. Import this year's class lists so your roster is real. See Manage students.
  4. Check instruments out to students. See Checking out & in.

You cannot break it by clicking around. Nothing is deleted without asking you first, and anything you archive (students, old instruments) can be brought back. Have a look through the screens before you put real data in.

Bringing in your old data

A one time job when you first set up. You do not repeat it every year.

Roka can read the exports from the programs BSD music teachers already use:

FromWhat comes across
CutTime (.xlsx)Your instrument inventory
Charms (.csv)Inventory plus repair history, vendors and purchase order details that CutTime does not export
Synergy (.csv)Class lists, one file per class

How to export a class list from Synergy

  1. In Synergy, open the class you want.
  2. Export the student list as a CSV file and save it somewhere you can find it, like your Downloads folder.
  3. In Roka, use the import screen, pick the file, and tell it which class and period the file belongs to.
  4. Roka works out for itself whether a file came from CutTime or from Charms, so it does not ask you.

Synergy puts one row per parent or guardian, so a student with two parents appears twice in the file. Roka expects that and merges them into one student with two contacts. You do not need to clean the file up first.

Import one class at a time. Each import asks which class and period it is for, and that is what puts students in the right roster, seating chart and agenda. Importing everything as one lump leaves you sorting it out by hand later.

Equipment inventory

Every school owned instrument, where it is, and what state it is in.

Finding things

Type in the search box to filter the list. The status filter narrows it to what is available or checked out. Columns ▼ lets you choose which columns you want to see, and Show Inactive brings back instruments you have retired.

Adding and editing

  • ➕ adds an instrument.
  • ✏️ Edit changes the one you have selected.

Worth filling in, because other parts of the program use them:

  • Description, for example "Trumpet - Bb" or "Viola". This is what groups instruments together when Roka offers a replacement.
  • Size, for strings. A 3/4 cello and a 4/4 cello are different instruments to a growing student.
  • Barcode, district number or serial number. Any one of these makes two identical looking flutes tellable apart everywhere in the program.
  • Condition and comments. If you write words like "unrepairable", "lost", "missing", "stolen" or "retired" here, Roka stops offering that instrument to students.
  • Year made, if you know it, or can work it out from the serial number.

The tabs at the bottom

With an instrument selected: Details is everything on record, Checkout History is every student who has ever had it, and Repairs is what has been done to it and what that cost.

Exports

📊 Exports ▾ gives you:

  • Entire inventory, as Excel
  • Instruments needing repair
  • Currently checked out
  • Barcode sheet for brass and woodwind, as a PDF to print
  • Repair cost report, sorted by what you have spent
  • Full repair details

Checking instruments out and in

One student at a time

  1. Select the instrument.
  2. Press 📤 Check Out.
  3. Pick the student from the list and check the return date. The list holds the children at that instrument's own school, so a Medina cello cannot go out to a child at Clyde Hill by mistake. Start typing to narrow it.
  4. Leave the rental fee ticked unless this student has already paid.

Checking in is the same, with 📥 Check In.

The rental fee

Elementary loans carry no fee, so at a 5th grade school there is nothing to pick, no fee is raised, and the loan form that prints says no charge will be made rather than quoting 75 dollars.

Checking an instrument out adds the school year rental fee (75 dollars unless you have changed it) to that student's account, one fee per instrument. A student who takes home two instruments owes two fees. You can see, change, waive or mark these paid in Budget, under Student Fees.

Summer rentals are a separate, cheaper fee (20 dollars unless you have changed it).

Other things on this screen

  • 🎒 Check Out Item is for the small things: a mute, a lyre, a method book.
  • 🏫 Loan to Another School records an instrument that has physically left your building. Loaned instruments are not offered to your students while they are out.
  • 📄 Generate Form prints the district Equipment Loan Form for the student to sign.
  • 📋 Checkouts lists everything currently out, and lets you return several at once.

Two students really can share one instrument, so Roka allows it. It warns you first, because most of the time it is a mistake.

Barcode scanning

For the first week of school, when the whole band is in a line.

With a handheld scanner plugged in, open Bulk Check Out / Check In from the Equipment screen. Scan the student's ID, then scan the instrument. Repeat. The scanner behaves like a keyboard, so there is nothing to set up.

Check Out + Form does the check out and prints the loan form in one go.

The barcode sheets

If your instruments or student cards do not already carry barcodes, print the two sheets once and keep them next to the computer:

  • Equipment screen, 📊 Exports ▾, Barcode Sheet for brass and woodwind.
  • Manage Students, 📤 Export ▾, Barcode Sheet to scan student IDs.

Repairs

🔧 Repair on the Equipment screen opens the repair center.

  • 📋 Upload Invoice reads a repair invoice PDF and matches the lines to your instruments. Check what it found before you accept it.
  • ➕ Add Repair records a repair by hand.
  • 🚚 Mark Out for Repair flags an instrument as away at the shop, so it is not offered to a student.
  • ✅ Mark Repaired brings it back.
  • 📊 Export This View saves whatever list you are looking at.

Repair costs feed the Budget screen on their own, so what you spend at the shop shows up in your year's spending without being typed twice. The repair cost report is the argument for replacing an instrument: it shows what each one has cost you over its life.

Carry over instruments to a new year

This is for middle and high school. A 5th grader does not do 5th grade again, so there is no last year to carry forward and the button is not on an elementary school's screen at all.

Most students keep the instrument they had, so the new year starts from last year's list instead of an empty screen.

🔁 Carry Over… on the Equipment screen.

Import this year's class lists first. Carry over only offers students it can confirm are on this year's roster. If you have not imported yet, it will tell you there is nothing to carry forward, which is correct rather than broken.

What you see

One row per instrument a student finished last year holding. For each row: who they are, this year's grade, what they had, and what they will get.

  • Students who graduated or did not come back are not listed at all. Your outgoing top year has moved on to the high school and their instruments return to your shelf.
  • A repair or a swap counts once. If a student went through two clarinets last year because one went to the shop, that is one clarinet here, not two.
  • A student who genuinely holds two instruments gets two rows, and owes two rental fees.

Changing what a student gets

  • The dropdown offers every free instrument of the same kind. If two look identical, they are numbered, for example "Flute (2 of 3)".
  • An instrument already out to somebody else is marked with a ⚠ and offered last. It is allowed, but look twice.
  • An instrument the student kept over the summer says "still has it". That loan simply continues, so nothing is checked out twice.
  • String players who have grown get a ⬆ button on their row to move up a size. Orchestra teachers get it on every sized instrument.

Fees

Every ticked row is billed the school year rental, one per instrument. Untick the fee box at the top if you have already billed these.

Running the screen a second time is safe. Anything already assigned this year comes up unticked, so nobody is charged twice.

Sheet music

Your library, and what you have performed.

Adding music

  • Add Music types one piece in by hand.
  • Import Music reads photographs or PDFs. You can photograph a stack of folder covers laid out on a table, or single score pages, and it pulls out titles, composers and arrangers. Always look over what it found before saving.
  • 📄 Import Program reads an old concert program and adds everything on it to your performance history in one go. This is the quick way to record years of past concerts.

Performances

The Performances tab records when a piece was played and by which ensemble, so you can see at a glance whether you have programd something recently. 🎼 Performances by Ensemble shows it the other way round.

The scanning tools

Process OMR reads printed notation into a MusicXML file, and Validate with LLM checks the result for the mistakes music scanning usually makes. These are for digitising a score, not for cataloguing, and are slower than the rest of the program.

If a description looks like it has code in it, see Tidy AI text.

Tidy AI text

🧹 Tidy AI Text on the Sheet Music screen.

When the AI looks a piece up online, it marks which sentence came from which source. Those marks are meant for a program that turns them into footnotes at the bottom of a page. Roka puts the description straight into the notes field instead, so for a while the marks came through as they were, and a description read like this:

<cite index="4-14">A Duke Ellington classic with limited technical demands.</cite> • <cite index="7-1">Grade 2</cite> level

when what it should say is:

A Duke Ellington classic with limited technical demands. • Grade 2 level

Fixing the ones already in your library

  1. Open Sheet Music and press 🧹 Tidy AI Text.
  2. You get a list of every affected piece, showing what it says now and what it would say afterwards.
  3. Everything starts ticked. Untick anything you would rather leave.
  4. Press Tidy Ticked.

Only the tags are removed. Every word inside them is kept exactly as it reads, so nothing you or the AI wrote is lost. If there is nothing to fix, the tool says so and changes nothing.

Roka also offers this the first time you open Sheet Music after finding affected pieces, so you do not have to know the button is there.

New imports no longer pick the tags up, so this is a one off for anything brought in before. If you ever see them again, please report it.

Uniforms and attire

The same idea as instruments, for clothing.

You define your own garment types, so this works for concert dresses, tuxedo jackets, marching bibbers, shakos, choir robes or anything else you hand out. Use 🏷️ Garment Types to set them up.

  • One piece goes to one student. Unlike instruments, a garment cannot be shared, and Roka will not let it be.
  • No fee is charged when a uniform is checked out.
  • Sizing up is suggested when a returning student needs the next size.
  • 📥 Import reads a spreadsheet of garments, and will match any that are already assigned to a student.

The who has what chart

One row per student, one column per garment type, showing the item number they hold. Blanks are the missing pieces. This is the sheet to print and post so students who forget their number can look it up instead of asking you.

Uniform hand out is the job to give a parent volunteer. See Helper Mode.

Manage students

Your roster, one school year at a time.

Getting students in

📥 Import ▾ takes a district class roster CSV. Import one class at a time and tell it the class and period. It also has an option to update instruments from another school's CSV, for when a feeder school sends you their outgoing students.

Roka reads the columns by their headings, so it does not matter which fields you chose to include in the export or what order they came out in. At a 5th grade school you are not asked for a class period, because there are none.

➕ Add Student types one in by hand.

Working with the list

  • 🏷️ Assign puts several selected students into an ensemble or class period at once. For more than five it shows you what it is about to do, to how many, and names the first few. Ticks clear themselves once an assignment lands, so the violins you did a moment ago cannot come along with the violas.
  • ✉️ Email List builds an address list for the students you have selected, ready to paste into Outlook.
  • 🗑️ Delete removes everyone you have ticked, not just the row you have highlighted. It asks first, saying how many and naming the first few. A student still holding an instrument is kept back and named, so one unreturned trumpet does not stop you clearing a whole roster.
  • 🗂️ Inactive Students shows students you have archived, and brings any of them back. Nothing you delete is really gone, it moves here.
  • The Instrument History tab shows everything a student has ever had out.

Exports

  • Student list, as Excel
  • Numbers per part, which tells you how many copies of each part to make. Tick the classes that are playing the piece and read the count.
  • Outgoing students for high school directors, so the next school starts the year knowing who is coming and what they play
  • Barcode sheet of student IDs, to print

Students are never deleted when a year ends, only marked inactive. Their history stays attached to them.

5th grade

If you teach 5th grade band or orchestra, each school you are posted to keeps its own instruments and its own children. Nothing is shared between them.

Add your schools in Settings ▸ Schools. For each one, say whether it is elementary and whether you run band or orchestra there. Elementary loans carry no rental fee, so that is switched off for you.

A 🎺 5th Grade button then appears on the main screen, with a tab per school. If you teach only elementary, your schools appear on the main screen as buttons instead, and Sheet Music and Uniforms are hidden.

What an elementary school does not have. No class periods, only sections. No rental fee, on the loan or on the form that prints. No carry over, because a 5th grader does not do 5th grade twice. Roka leaves all three off those screens rather than asking you to ignore them.

Inside a school's tab

  • Instruments and Students work exactly as they do for a middle or high school program, but show only that school.
  • 📥 Import Inventory From Another Teacher loads the file the previous teacher saved when they handed the school on. It is safe to run twice: anything already there is left alone.
  • 🗄 Import From CutTime / Charms is for your first year, bringing a cupboard across from whatever came before. Roka works out which of the two a file came from, so it does not ask.
  • 🎓 Import Class List (CSV) loads this year's children. Import each section separately. They arrive belonging to this school, which is what lets you lend them its instruments.
  • 📦 Hand Over This School saves the inventory, checkout history and repair history in one file for whoever teaches here next.

Sections and choir

Most schools run two sections back to back, so a child is in Section 1 or Section 2, never both. Some schools also run a choir before or after school; tick Also sings in choir on the child, or, if the whole year group is in it, tick Everyone here is in choir on the school and every child imported joins automatically.

What you cannot do, on purpose

There is no view showing every school at once, and an instrument can only be checked out to a child at the same school. With up to six schools on the go, acting on the wrong one is the mistake worth preventing, so the arrangement that allows it does not exist. Emails are the same: you can only write to the school whose tab you are on.

At the end of the year

📦 New School Year… covers elementary too, and covers both halves in one pass if you teach secondary as well. It exports one inventory file per school to hand on, plus a single combined list of everything awaiting repair for the district coordinator, and lets you add the schools you are picking up and archive the ones you are handing over.

Archiving a school keeps everything. Tick Show archived on the Schools tab to see them and ↩ Restore one later. A restored school comes back with its instruments but an empty roster: the children who were in its 5th grade have moved on to middle school, so you import a fresh class list.

Starting a new school year

🧰 Teacher Tools ▸ 📦 New School Year…. Do this once, in the autumn. It used to sit on the main screen, where it was too easy to press by accident.

  1. Choose the new year. It suggests the next one.
  2. Import this year's class lists, one CSV per class. Returning students are rolled forward, keeping their instrument, contacts and history, and move up a grade. Names it does not recognize become new students.
  3. Archive whoever is left. This runs when you press Finish, after the imports, so it only archives students who are not on any new class list: your graduating year, and anyone who dropped. Nothing is deleted.
  4. Roll uniforms forward. Returning students keep the pieces they had. The option only releases gear still held by students who did not come back.

Afterwards, go to Carry Over on the Equipment screen to hand instruments back out.

Honors and Junior All State marks reset every year, because they are earned again each year.

A student who is a year ahead of their classmates, for example a sixth grader in the intermediate band, is normal and handled. They simply appear on the class list you import them on.

Budget and student fees

A school year runs 1 July to 30 June. The Budget window has its own year selector.

Money in and out

Expenses and income are split by where the money comes from (Building, ASB, Boosters or Other), and you can make your own categories with 🏷️ Categories. Repair costs are pulled in automatically, so you do not enter them twice.

🚌 Field Trip adds the costs of a trip as one entry. 📊 Export Excel saves the whole year for whoever asks for it.

Student fees

🎽 Student Fees is where instrument rentals, polo shirts and anything else a student owes is tracked. You can:

  • Mark fees ✅ Paid, ⛔ Unpaid or 🚫 Waive them
  • Add a fee to a whole class at once
  • ✉️ Email Unpaid to build a reminder to the families who still owe
  • Change what each fee type costs with Manage Fee Types

Instrument rentals appear here on their own when you check an instrument out. If a student is playing their own instrument, waive or remove the fee here.

Seating charts

  1. Choose the class period, or combine ensembles for a concert.
  2. Set how many seats are in each row and how you want students sorted.
  3. Save the chart so it comes back next time.

Rows are color coded to match velcro carpet markers. 📋 Copy Image and 💾 Save Image… get the chart out for printing or for a substitute.

The set up buttons

  • 🎼 Concert Setup… lays students out in concert arcs instead of classroom rows.
  • 🎷 Jazz Setup… uses a jazz stage layout.
  • 🚫 Keep-Apart Pairs… keeps two students who should not sit together apart, every time you reshuffle.
  • ♿ Special Accommodations… holds a student to the front or back row.
  • 🎺 Change a student's instrument… fixes a wrong instrument without leaving the chart.
  • 🔀 Shuffle… gives you a fresh arrangement, obeying everything above.

Percussion rotation

So the same student is not on the drum set every day.

Enter the percussionists in a class and Roka builds a balanced daily rotation through the seats: mallets, snare, timpani and auxiliary, plus bass drum and, for your older groups, drum set. Over a full cycle everybody gets a fair share, with beginners starting on mallets.

  • 🎓 From Student List… pulls the percussionists straight off your roster.
  • 🎛 Rotation Stations… changes which seats are in play.
  • 🎚 Limit Rotation… keeps a particular student off a particular station.
  • ⭐ Special Day… overrides one day, for a concert or an assembly.
  • 🗓 Full Grid shows the whole cycle at once, which is the version to print.
  • 🎵 Mallet Equipment… records which mallets go with which instrument.

Jazz rhythm section

On the Percussion tab, if you run a jazz band.

A jazz rhythm section is not fixed the way a concert percussion section is, so you tell Roka what it is: list the seats in play (drum set, piano, bass, guitar, vibraphone, or anything else you want, including a doubled tenor sax), then enter each player and tick which seats they can cover.

  • You can run more than one jazz band, each with its own seats and players.
  • 🎛 Part limits… caps how many players sit on a part at once.
  • 🎼 Edit Lineup sets who plays what on a particular song.

Concerts

One place to plan a concert cycle.

  1. ➕ New Concert: date, time, place, and which ensembles are playing.
  2. 🎼 Repertoire: what each ensemble is playing. Fine to leave empty early on and fill in later.
  3. 📄 Details Page: the family handout with attire, arrival time, required rehearsals and the itinerary.
  4. 📖 Program (.pub): builds a real Publisher document you can edit, not a locked PDF.
  5. ✉ Reminders: emails to families at two weeks, one week and two days out. ✉ Open in Outlook sends them straight from Roka, families in BCC. See Sending emails.
  • 🎖 Honors records Honors and Junior All State students for the program.
  • 📊 Export Roster (Excel)… gives the office a simple who is out sheet for a school day performance.
  • 📋 Use as Template for a New Concert… copies last year's concert as the starting point for this year's.

Use the arrow to the left of a concert's date to fold it away while you work on another one. Editing a concert leaves you where you were in the list rather than jumping you back to the top.

Field trips

Everything BSD procedure 2320P asks for, in the order it asks for it.

Setting one up

  • ➕ New Field Trip: who is going, when, where, how you are travelling, and what it costs. Say whether it is a day trip or an overnight one, because the district asks for different things for each.
  • Tick this is an elementary school trip for a 5th grade group. Middle and high school day trips use FinalForms instead of paper permission slips, elementary trips do not.
  • 📋 Copy From Previous… starts from a trip you have already run and brings every answer with it, including your own checklist columns.

The district application

📄 Application opens the whole district form in one window. Fill it in, save it, and come back to it as often as you like. Generate PDF of completed form prints it ready to sign.

  • The number of students participating is counted from your roster.
  • The substitute cost is the district rate you tick, not a figure you work out.
  • Total Trip Cost adds up whatever you have entered as figures. Type N/A where there is nothing to pay and it counts as zero. Type TBD where you do not know yet and the total says what it is waiting on, for example 266.00 plus transportation. Until at least one line holds a figure the total stays empty rather than guessing.

Deadlines and dates

  • Every deadline is counted in school weeks, the way the district counts them, so winter break does not eat your notice period.
  • An overnight trip needs school board approval. Pick the board meeting you are aiming for and the tick box shows the date, for example School board approval (expected Sept 17). Only regular meetings are offered, and the packet is due to your principal four school weeks before it.
  • Day trips do not go to the board, whatever the age group.
  • Roka warns about blackout dates: the day before a break, the first week of a semester, the week before exams, and AP testing. A trip in spring break itself is not flagged, since those are usually the good ones.
  • Overdue deadlines show in red, ones coming up soon in amber.

Who is going, and what they have handed in

  • 👥 Roster & Forms: tick who is going, and tick off each form as it comes back. Untick Going for a child who is staying behind and they drop to the bottom of the list, greyed out, with nothing more chased up. Tick them back on if they change their mind.
  • Overnight trips track the paper Parent Authorization form. Elementary day trips track the paper permission slip. Middle and high school day trips need neither, because FinalForms covers them.
  • Add Checklist Item makes a column of your own, for an interest survey, a deposit, a uniform, anything. Rename or delete it later without losing the ticks.
  • The status line counts what is still outstanding, both the district forms and your own columns.

Printing and sending

  • Permission forms prints one PDF with a page per student, the school portion already filled in.
  • Student list exports to Excel with the header frozen and filters on, for the office or the nurse.
  • 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Chaperones: parent chaperones with contacts filled in from your student data, and the one adult per ten students count done for you. ⟳ Fill Missing Contacts chases up the gaps.
  • ✉ Reminders: emails for families, chaperones and the teachers whose classes lose students that day, opened straight in Outlook. See Sending emails. The staff email carries the student list as a table.

Use the arrow to the left of a trip's date to fold it away. Editing a trip leaves you where you were in the list rather than jumping you back to the top.

Trip costs can be pushed into the Budget so the money side stays in one place.

Daily agendas

What goes on the board, for each class, each day.

Pick the class and the date. Roka suggests a sensible agenda for that day built around your concert dates and repertoire, with the warm up pitched at the right level for how close the concert is. Change anything you like, and what you change is what sticks.

  • 🖥 Present puts the agenda on the projector, full screen, with a clock and a timer.
  • ⧉ Copy Previous Day when today is yesterday again.
  • ↺ Reset Day throws away your edits and goes back to the suggestion.
  • 📷 Paste Image puts a picture on the board, for example a fingering chart or a rhythm to clap.
  • 🎯 Assessments… notes what you are assessing that day.
  • Classes with a percussion rotation show today's rotation beside the agenda, so the section can see where they are without asking.
  • The banner shows how long until this class next performs, counted from its own concerts on the planner, not another class's.

A class with more than one section

Tell Roka in 🗂 Manage Classes…: the Periods box takes one period per section, so typing “1, 2” means the class runs twice and the agenda gets a P1 / P2 toggle. The plan is written once and shared; the toggle switches only what really differs by period.

With two secondary schools, Manage Classes also has a School column. Bind each class to its school and concerts, field trips, seating charts, percussion rotations and agendas all pull that class's roster from the right building, even if the other school has a similarly named class. Two classes with the same name at different schools are refused; name them apart.

  • Each period keeps its own percussion rotation (matched by period on the 🥁 Percussion tab) and its own Missing lists.
  • Any line or picture can be marked P1 only with the small All / P1 / P2 chooser on its row. Use it for a section's own seating chart, or for naming the kids in one period who still owe a playing assignment. Untagged items show for every section, which is the usual case.
  • The toggle is in Present too, so switching periods between classes does not mean leaving the projector.

Arranging the day

  • The ▲ and ▼ on each line move it up or down, pictures included.

District no school days are already known, so holidays are skipped.

Sending emails

Anywhere Roka writes an email for you, one button opens it in Outlook, already addressed and filled in.

You will find ✉ Open in Outlook on the concert reminders, the field trip reminders, the unpaid fee reminder, and the student email list. There is nothing to copy and nothing to paste.

Who it goes to

LineWho
ToYou. So the message is addressed to somebody, you get your own copy of exactly what went out, and if a parent hits Reply All it comes back to you instead of to every family.
BCCThe families. Always BCC, never To, so no family sees any other family's address.

Set your own address in Settings, under Teacher. The first time you send, Roka asks for it and remembers it.

The staff and facilities email is the exception: colleagues go in the To line, where they can see each other and reply to the group.

Your wording is kept

This is the part worth knowing:

  • Edit the wording in Roka, in the box on screen. When you press ✉ Open in Outlook, what is in that box is saved as this concert's or trip's email, and the same text is what opens in Outlook. There is no separate save step.
  • Next time starts from your version, not from the generated one. For a trip it is reused for both reminder stages and carried into next year by 📋 Copy From Previous.
  • Changes you make in Outlook after it opens are for that one message. That is usually what you want, since a lot of last-minute edits are one-offs. If you rewrite it properly in Outlook and want to keep that version, paste it back into the Roka box and press the button again.
  • ↺ Reset to Auto throws your version away and goes back to the generated text.

Short version: Roka is where the reusable wording lives, Outlook is where the message goes out. Reword it in Roka and it is yours from then on.

If Outlook is not on the computer

Roka falls back to whatever mail program the computer uses. Those links have a length limit, so with a very long address list the message still opens but the addresses are copied to the clipboard instead, and Roka tells you to paste them into BCC. The copy buttons are all still there either way.

Helper Mode (for parent volunteers)

Hand the computer to a volunteer without handing over your students' information.

In Helper Mode everything is hidden except uniform check out, and student contact details are hidden even there. To get back to full access you have to enter the Helper PIN, which is why the volunteer must not know it.

  1. Set a Helper PIN in Settings, under Teacher.
  2. Switch into Helper Mode from the main screen. Every open window closes.
  3. The volunteer checks uniforms in and out.
  4. Press 🔒 Exit Helper Mode and enter the PIN.

Sharing with a co-director

Off unless you turn it on. Most teachers never need this.

Some large high schools have two directors who keep separate class lists but share one instrument inventory, check out log, repair log and music library. This connects two computers to one shared copy of those, while each director's students, seating charts, concerts and agendas stay their own.

  • One director sets it up and gets a code.
  • The other enters that code in Settings, under Sharing.
  • You need to be online to make changes to shared items. Everything else works offline as normal.

Ask Reginald and the AI features

🎩 Ask Reginald, on the Equipment and Sheet Music screens, answers questions about your own inventory and library in plain language. Reginald is a grumpy retired butler, and he knows it.

The AI is also what reads photographs of music, repair invoices and old concert programs.

Always check what it produces before you save it. Reading a photograph of a handwritten folder label is a guess, and it is quicker to correct one field now than to find a wrong title in your library in two years.

These features need an internet connection. If they are not set up, or the connection is out, the rest of the program carries on working normally.

Backups and profiles

Backups

Set an external backup folder in Settings, under Backup, and point it somewhere that is not this computer: a OneDrive folder or a USB stick. Roka backs up when you close it, and Back Up Now does it on demand.

A backup folder on the same computer is not a backup. If the laptop is lost or wiped, so is everything.

Profiles

Each teacher on a computer gets a profile, and each profile is a completely separate set of data: its own instruments, students, music and settings. Switch Profile at the bottom of the main screen moves between them.

Report a bug or ask for a feature

Please look through this guide first. Use the search box at the top left, or the list of sections. Most questions are answered somewhere on this page, and checking first means the real problems get attention sooner.

If it is not here, the buttons below open a new email in Outlook with the right questions already in it. Fill in the blanks and send.

Report a bug Request a feature

When reporting a bug

A screenshot is worth more than a paragraph. Press Windows + Shift + S, drag a box around the problem, then press Ctrl + V in the email to paste it in.

Then say:

  • Which screen you were on
  • What you did, step by step, so it can be repeated
  • What you expected to happen
  • What happened instead
  • Whether it happens every time, or only sometimes

"The seating chart is broken" cannot be fixed. "On the Seating Charts tab, I picked period 3, pressed Shuffle, and two students disappeared from the chart, every time" can be fixed the same day.

When asking for a feature

Be as detailed as you can:

  • What you want to be able to do
  • How you do it today, on paper, in a spreadsheet or in another program
  • Who would use it and how often
  • What it would save you
  • Anything that would help: a sample of the form you use now, or a screenshot of how another program does it

Student privacy

Roka holds the information a music teacher needs to run a music program: names, grade, class, instrument, guardian contacts and what a family owes.

It is not going to be expanded to hold sensitive student data. That means no health information, no special education or 504 documentation, no discipline records, no counselling notes, and no detailed instructional modifications. Student privacy law is the reason, and it is not a matter of effort or priority.

Where the program has to know something in this area, it is deliberately kept shallow. A seating chart can be told that a student needs to sit at the front, because that is a seating fact. It does not record why, and it should not be told why.

Please keep that in mind when asking for features. A request for anything that would put protected information into this program cannot be built, no matter how useful it would be.

Common questions

My students have all disappeared.

Almost always the year, not the data. Check which school year the screen is showing. Manage Students and Teacher Tools each have their own year selector, and the Budget window has another. Nothing is deleted when a year rolls over, it is archived, and 🗂️ Inactive Students will show you.

Carry Over says there is nothing to carry forward.

This year's class lists have not been imported yet. Carry over only offers students it can confirm are on this year's roster. Import the class lists, then come back. See Carry over.

A student was charged twice for one instrument.

Open Budget, then Student Fees, find the extra line and remove or waive it. Fees are one per instrument, so a student holding two really does owe two.

Two of my instruments look identical in the list.

Give them a barcode, district number or serial number in ✏️ Edit. Until then, Roka numbers them, for example "Flute (2 of 3)", so you can still tell them apart when assigning.

An instrument I do not want to lend keeps being offered.

Put a word like "unrepairable", "lost", "missing", "stolen" or "retired" in its condition or comments, and it will stop being offered.

Can I use this at home and at school?

Your data lives on the computer you are using. Set a backup folder in OneDrive on both machines so the same data follows you. Do not run both at once on the same folder.

Something crashed and I lost my place.

Reopen the program. Work is saved as you go, not when you close. Then please report it: Report a bug.
